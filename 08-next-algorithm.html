Next algorithm — line up the top corners

The top face is yellow, but the corners on the sides are still in the wrong places. This algorithm swaps corners around until every corner sits where it belongs.

Goal: every top corner is in its correct place — its three colours match the three faces they touch. (The top face stays yellow throughout.)

The algorithm from the document

Left, top reverse, right reverse, top, left reverse, top twice, right, top reverse, right reverse, top twice, right.

Eleven moves (thirteen letters in the sheet's notation, because T T is one double turn). It swaps two corners of the top layer — the two on the right — and, as a side effect, two top edges.

How to use it

  1. Keep the yellow face on top.
  2. Look at the top corners and find the two that need to change places.
  3. Turn the cube so those two corners are on the right side, then run the algorithm.
  4. Check again. Turn the top (T) to bring the corners you still need to swap to the right, and run it again.
  5. Stop when all four top corners are in their correct places.

How to tell a corner is “correct”

Look at one corner of the top layer. It has three stickers. The corner is in the right place when its three colours are the same as the three centres around it — for example yellow, green and red on the corner between the yellow, green and red faces.

The corner may still be twisted — that is fine here, because the whole top face is already yellow.

Note on the sheet. This page and the last one come from a handwritten sheet, so the moves were read carefully and each algorithm was checked on a cube model to make sure it leaves the finished layers alone. If your cube ever looks worse after a run, check you did the whole sequence — most mistakes are a missed or reversed turn.
