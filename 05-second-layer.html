The upside-down T — solving the second layer

The first layer stays solved and we fill in the four edges of the middle layer. The trick is to make an upside-down T first, then drop the edge in.

Goal: the bottom two layers finished — first layer plus the four middle edges, with every side face's bottom two rows matching its centre.

Find an edge with no yellow

The middle-layer edges are the four edges that have no yellow sticker. Look for one in the top layer and turn the top (T) until its side colour matches the centre under it. Now the edge, the centre and the solved bottom row line up into a T shape pointing down.

Front face — the upside-down T

Then check the edge's other colour — the one on top. That tells you which way the edge must travel:

  • if it has to go to the right, use the right algorithm;
  • if it has to go to the left, use the left algorithm.

Send it right → T R T1 R1 · T1 F1 T F

Top, right, top reverse, right reverse — top reverse, front reverse, top, front.

Send it left → T1 L1 T L · T F T1 F1

Top reverse, left reverse, top, left — top, front, top reverse, front reverse.

Stuck edge? Sometimes a middle-layer edge is already in the middle layer but the wrong way round. Hold it on the right and run the right algorithm once — that pops it out to the top. Then make the upside-down T and insert it properly.
Repeat for all four middle edges. When you finish, the bottom two layers are complete and only the top layer is left.
